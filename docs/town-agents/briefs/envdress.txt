YOUR ROLE: `envdress`. You make the town's streets and harbor feel lived-in: the human-scale dressing on top of the structural world. This is half of the env role in TOWN.md section 8; the other agent is `env`.

YOUR FILES: src/models/env-harbor.js and src/models/env-life.js. Both exist as stubs that already state the call contract; keep it. Edit nothing else.
- Use these read-only: helpers from env-kit.js (furniture, addSolid / solidRect, ChunkBuilder layers, SignAtlas), streetGeo() from env-streets.js, and createBusStop from props-town.js.
- If you need a helper that does not exist, write it in your own file. Do not edit env-kit.js.

HOW YOU PLUG IN
- `env` (in environment.js) calls `buildHarbor(env)` and `buildStreetLife(env)` with `env = { root, S, F, G, signs, rnd, geo }`.
- S, F and G are ChunkBuilders:
  - S: solid lit geometry, casts shadows;
  - F: flat ground paint and decals;
  - G: glowing parts, such as lamp heads and lit windows.
- `signs` is the shared SignAtlas: about 15 signs for you; env sizes it.
- `rnd` is seeded random. `geo` holds the street edges (hz0, hz1, mz0, mz1, wx0 ..., quayZ, hbN).
- Push static geometry into S / F / G. Moving things (boats bobbing, a flag, a turning wind vane) are separate small meshes added to `env.root` and animated from the `update(dt, t)` you return. Nothing may be allocated per frame.
- Solid things inside walkable areas (fountain, kiosks, big planters, dumpsters) get registered with addSolid / solidRect. Thin things (lamp posts, signs, bollards) do not.
- env wires your two calls into environment.js early in its run; watch /tmp/wb-progress/env.md for it. Until then, test with a temporary local page of your own (for example src/models/preview-dress.html + .js, which you may create) that builds a ChunkBuilder set, calls your functions and renders with the game camera. Once env's wiring is in, use `?nogame&cam=x,z` on the real page.
